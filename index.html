DECIMAG · Right-to-Act

Authorization is not the same as the right to act

DECIMAG represents the organization as a computational structure. Before execution, it evaluates each Proposed Action against the organization’s current structural reality.

Proposed Actions can come from AI agents or enterprise systems.

The missing decision

Access answers one question.
Execution requires another.

Identity, access and policy systems establish who may reach a system. They also determine which permissions that person or system holds.

A permitted action can still conflict with the organization as it exists now. Its structure, responsibilities, dependencies and current conditions may not support that action.

DECIMAG evaluates whether the Proposed Action has the Right-to-Act in that Organizational Reality.

Enterprise context

Designed to sit alongside systems you already use

DECIMAG complements identity, access, workflow and security systems. These systems can provide context and receive outcomes. DECIMAG does not replace them.

Security may provide an input to a Right-to-Act decision. A security system may receive the outcome, and the outcome may have a security consequence. Security does not define the DECIMAG product category.
